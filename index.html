School of Tomorrow's AI · Wetzikon — Zürich, Switzerland

Strange questions,
made material.

School of Tomorrow's AI is an artistic research platform. It turns strange questions about science, technology and possible futures into experiments, prototypes and public experiences. AI, like other scientific and technical systems, is treated as material — with grain, resistance and surprise.

What it
investigates

The School works where artistic research meets science, technology and the body.

01

Strange questions

The work starts from questions that do not fit a product brief or a standard lab protocol. What happens if a room becomes an instrument, a garden listens, a gesture becomes a drawing.

Artistic Research
02

Science and technology as material

Sensors, code, circuits, models and scientific methods are handled like any other material: tested, stressed, combined, allowed to fail. The point is not to master a tool. The point is to see what the material does.

Speculative AI
03

Embodied systems

Bodies, biosignals, machine perception, biological systems and spatial interaction are the field. Inquiry happens through movement, touch, voice, signal and presence — not only through screens.

Embodied Systems Biosignals Machine Perception
04

Possible futures

The School treats the future as something that can be prototyped and encountered, not predicted. Small experiments allow a future condition to be tested before it becomes a scenario.

02

How it
works

The School does not stop at describing a problem. A question is built, placed in a room, and watched.

01

Speculative prototyping

A prototype is a way of thinking. It can be rough, unstable or incomplete, as long as it makes the question physical.

02

Making and experimentation

Research proceeds by assembling materials, running tests, breaking the setup and adjusting. Analysis develops alongside the object.

03

Participation and public testing

Some experiments stay in the atelier. Others are opened to a public, a partner or a live situation to see how the system behaves when other bodies enter it.

Artist &
Research Lead

Lina Lopes

Artist, educator,
data scientist.

Lina Lopes is a Brazilian artist, educator and data scientist based in Zürich, Switzerland. Her practice moves between interactive installation, machine learning, biosignals and embodied systems, using science and technology as material for artistic research.

She founded School of Tomorrow's AI as a platform for speculative prototyping, experimentation and public research — a place where strange questions can become physical, testable and shared.

The
base

The Atelier
Schönaustrasse 13 8620 Wetzikon, Zürich
Open in Google Maps ↗︎

The School is based in an atelier at Schönau Areal in Wetzikon, in the Zürich region of Switzerland.

The atelier functions as the School's research and prototyping base: a place for building, testing, artistic research and public experiments. It includes spaces for hands-on fabrication, movement, experimentation and working with other people.

Shared atelier

A former factory used for research, building and tests. Its industrial rooms are infrastructure, not a campus.

Space for making

Studio, dirty workshop, dance floor and gathering rooms — the spatial tools of the work.

Public encounters

Experiments start here. Some stay in the atelier. Others leave as public encounters with other bodies in the room.

The School collaborates with cultural institutions, funders, research labs, foundations and organisations interested in unusual experimental research. Collaboration begins with a shared question.

01

Fund a research question

02

Commission an experiment

Commission a prototype, artwork or experimental research process around a question relevant to your context.

03

Propose a collaboration

Bring a scientific, technological, cultural or institutional setting into a shared investigation. The School enters as an artistic research partner.
