Pepper's Ghost

An old illusion made with light, reflection and a screen.

Pepper's Ghost is an optical illusion popularized in the 19th century by John Henry Pepper, based on an earlier design by Henry Dircks. An angled transparent surface reflects a hidden illuminated scene into view.

This small version uses the same principle. Instead of a stage, it uses your phone screen. Instead of a large glass panel, it uses four small transparent surfaces.

How it works

The phone displays four versions of the same animation. Each transparent side reflects one version toward the center. Your eyes perceive those reflections as a small floating image.

A short history

Henry Dircks proposed an earlier optical design. John Henry Pepper popularized the stage illusion in the nineteenth century, and it took his name.

The original version used angled glass and a hidden, brightly lit scene. The audience saw a figure that appeared to occupy the stage, though it was only a reflection.

This phone version uses the same basic reflection principle at a tiny scale: four images, four transparent faces, one floating ghost.
